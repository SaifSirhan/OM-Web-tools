01 — WHAT IS A PROJECT

A project is an endeavor to accomplish a specific objective through a unique set of interrelated activities and the effective utilization of resources.

Attributes

  • Clear objective
  • Series of interdependent activities
  • Various resources
  • Specific time frame
  • Unique, one-time endeavor
  • Sponsor or customer
  • Degree of uncertainty

02 — PROJECT OBJECTIVE

A clear statement of what is to be accomplished, agreed upon by the customer and the project team.

Defined in terms of

  • End product or deliverable
  • Schedule
  • Budget

03 — PROJECT CONSTRAINTS

Successful accomplishment of the project objective is constrained by these eight factors:

ScopeWhat gets done
QualityHow well

BudgetAt what cost

04 — PROJECT LIFE CYCLE

01 Initiating Identify need, problem, or opportunity. Develop project charter. Decide if RFP is needed.
02 Planning Build the baseline plan: scope, activities, resources, schedule, budget, risks.
03 Performing Accomplish the objective. Monitor and control progress. Manage changes with sponsor approval.
04 Closing Final payments. Evaluate staff. Post-project evaluation. Lessons learned. Archive documents.

Effort rises through initiating → planning → performing, peaks late in performing, and drops sharply through closing. Baseline plan is drawn at the planning/performing boundary; accepted deliverables at closing.

05 — PLANNING PROCESS

  1. Establish project objective
  2. Define scope
  3. Create WBS
  4. Assign responsibility
  5. Define specific activities
  6. Sequence activities
  7. Estimate activity resources
  8. Estimate activity durations
  9. Develop project schedule
  10. Estimate activity costs
  11. Determine budget

06 — PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Planning, organizing, coordinating, leading, and controlling resources to accomplish the project objective.

Two major functions

  • Establish a plan
  • Execute that plan

Three elements of executing

  • Perform the work
  • Monitor and control progress
  • Control changes

07 — STAKEHOLDERS

Anyone with a stake in the project — they want to be kept informed because of potential impact.

Who they are

  • Customer / sponsor
  • Project team (including subcontractors and suppliers)
  • Organizations or groups — supportive or adversarial

Stakeholder register fields

  • Key contact information
  • Role or specific topics of interest
  • Expectations
  • Known issues
  • Areas of potential influence
  • Issue log

08 — GLOBAL PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Six factors that change project dynamics

  • Cultural differences
  • Currency
  • Codes and regulations
  • Business organization
  • Political relations
  • Workforce availability

Helpful competencies

  • Foreign language skills
  • Knowledge of cultures, geography, world history, international economics

09 — BENEFITS

The ultimate benefit of implementing project management techniques is having a satisfied customer — whether you are the customer of your own project or a business being paid by a customer to perform a project.

Planning and communication are the two activities that most reliably prevent problems from occurring or minimize their impact when they do.

01 — PROJECT IDENTIFICATION & SELECTION

Projects start with recognizing a need, problem, or opportunity. Because organizations usually have more needs than resources, they must use a decision-making process to prioritize and select the project with the greatest overall benefit.

Selection Process

  • Evaluate needs, costs, and benefits
  • Develop criteria
  • List assumptions
  • Gather data
  • Evaluate each opportunity
  • Combine "gut" feelings with quantitative information

03 — REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL (RFP)

Used when an organization decides to outsource a project or major portion of it to an external contractor. It comprehensively describes the project requirements so contractors can submit a thorough proposal.

Guidelines for Developing an RFP

  • State project objective or purpose
  • Provide a statement of work
  • Include customer requirements
  • State deliverables the customer expects
  • State acceptance criteria
  • List customer-supplied items
  • State approvals required
  • State type of contract and payment terms
  • State schedule and key milestones
  • List format and content instructions
  • Indicate due date
  • Include evaluation criteria

04 — CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS

  • The need must be clearly defined before preparing an RFP.
  • Project selection should be based on which project provides the greatest overall benefit compared to costs.
  • A good RFP allows contractors to understand exactly what the customer expects.
  • An RFP should provide format and content instructions so the customer can fairly compare all proposals.
  • Never provide information to only some contractors; it gives an unfair competitive advantage.

01 — BUILDING RELATIONSHIPS

Building relationships with customers and partners is the foundation for successful funding and opportunities. Trust is key, and ethics are imperative.

Pre-RFP Efforts

  • Begin developing the relationship before the RFP is prepared
  • Helps the client identify needs
  • Positions the contractor to win the contract
  • Develops a more clearly focused response

02 — CREATING A WINNING PROPOSAL

A proposal is a selling document. It must highlight the best idea to solve the problem, emphasize benefits to the customer, and address all requirements from the RFP.

Three Main Sections

Technical Understand the need, proposed approach, benefits to customer.
Management Description of major tasks, deliverables, schedule, org, related experience.
Cost Estimated costs: labor, materials, equipment, facilities, travel, overhead, reserve, fee.

03 — CONTRACTS

A contract must be signed before starting work. It establishes communication and agreements of deliverables for a certain price.

Fixed Price Price remains fixed. Low risk for customer, high risk for contractor. For well-defined projects.
Cost-Reimbursement Price is actual costs. High risk for customer, low risk for contractor. For higher-risk projects.

04 — MEASURING PROPOSAL SUCCESS

  • Win Ratio: Number won / number submitted (all weighted equally).
  • Total Dollar Value: Value won / value submitted (more weight to larger values).

Do not bid on everything. Submit to win a fair share, but only respond where you have a better than average chance to win.
